1. Functional Description  
Computes the maximum number of disjoint pairs that can be formed from a sequence of nonnegative integer quantities, where each element can contribute pairs internally (two units from the same element) and, additionally, at most one unit may be taken from an element to pair with one unit taken from its immediate right neighbor (consuming one unit from each). Outputs the total number of pairs formed.

2. Inputs  
- An integer length value.  
- A sequence of that many integer quantities.

3. Outputs  
- A single integer: the total number of pairs formed under the rules described.

4. Preconditions  
- The length is within the supported bound (at most 100000).  
- The sequence provides at least that many quantities.  
- Quantities are assumed to be nonnegative integers.  
- The computed result fits within a signed 64-bit integer.

5. Postconditions  
- The output equals the maximum achievable number of pairs obtainable by repeatedly applying these allowed operations:  
  - Remove 2 units from any single position to form 1 pair.  
  - Remove 1 unit from a position and 1 unit from its immediate right neighbor to form 1 pair.  
- No unit is used in more than one pair.

6. Invariants  
- The running total of formed pairs is never decreased.  
- At all times, the remaining quantities (conceptually after forming pairs) are never negative.  
- Each cross-boundary pair, when formed, uses only adjacent positions and consumes exactly one unit from each of the two positions.

7. Error Handling / Exceptional Behavior  
- No explicit handling for invalid, missing, or out-of-range input. Behavior is unspecified if preconditions are violated.

8. State Transitions (if applicable)  
- Conceptual state: the multiset of remaining quantities across positions.  
- Transition rules: forming an internal pair reduces one position by 2; forming an adjacent pair reduces two neighboring positions by 1 each; the process ends when no further pairs can be formed.  
- The program outputs the final count of formed pairs and terminates.

9. Non-functional Constraints (if inferable)  
- Runs in time linear in the sequence length.  
- Uses memory proportional to the sequence length (bounded by the fixed maximum).
