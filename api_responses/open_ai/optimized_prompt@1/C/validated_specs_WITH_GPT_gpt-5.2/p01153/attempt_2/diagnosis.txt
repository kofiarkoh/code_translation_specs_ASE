A. Likely mismatch category  
Wrong transition rule / wrong definition of “join score” (wrong abstraction of what endpoints are used when combining segments on a circle).

B. What the current specification may be missing or misstating  
The specification describes each combine-step score as “the absolute difference between the values at the two boundary positions where the two segments meet.” That matches the generated code’s interpretation (difference across the split boundary inside the segment).  
But the source program’s DP transition does **not** use the two positions adjacent at the split boundary. Instead, for a segment starting at `i` of length `k+1`, when splitting into lengths `j+1` and `k-j`, it adds `abs(a[x] - a[i])` where `x` is the **start index of the right subsegment** (i.e., `i + j + 1` modulo `n`) and `i` is the **start index of the whole segment**. So the “join” is between the segment’s start and the right part’s start, not between the two touching boundary elements.

C. What general behavior should be clarified in the specification  
Clarify precisely which two positions’ values are used to compute the score added when two contiguous subsegments are combined in the DP/parenthesization. In particular, the combine-step score should be defined in terms of the segment decomposition used by the algorithm (which element represents each subsegment when combining), rather than assuming it is always the two adjacent boundary elements at the cut.

D. Why this clarification is supported by the source program  
In the source transition:

- `x = i + j + 1 (mod n)` selects the first index of the right subsegment.
- The added score is `abs(a[x] - a[i])`, involving `a[i]` (the start of the whole segment) and `a[x]` (the start of the right subsegment).
- The subproblem scores combined are `dp[i][j]` and `dp[x][k-j-1]`, confirming that `i` and `x` are the representative indices of the two parts being merged.

This is structurally different from the generated code’s rule `abs(a[i+k-1] - a[i+k])`, which uses the two adjacent elements at the split boundary. The sample failure (10 vs 12) is consistent with this systematic difference in the scoring rule, not a one-off edge case.
